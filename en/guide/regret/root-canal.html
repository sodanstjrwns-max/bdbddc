The two main reasons people regret Root Canal Treatment are ① Stopping once the pain subsides, leading to reinfection → even extraction; ② Delaying the post-treatment Crown until the tooth cracks and can no longer be savedThat’s it. 014 real reasons people regret Root Canal Treatment

Root Canal Treatment is the last chance to save a tooth before extraction last tooth-preserving treatmentThat’s why regret here can become 'regret over losing your natural tooth'.

Regret 1Stopping treatment — "I didn't go back because it didn't hurt"
Why does this happen?Root Canal Treatment usually takes 2–4 visits. Many people think they're "all better" after the first visit and don't come back. The temporary filling lasts only a few weeks; if bacteria get back in during that time, reinfection can occur — and it can ultimately lead to extraction.
PreventionPain gone ≠ treatment complete.Always finish the scheduled number of visits Keep coming as planned. If you're busy, plan the interval between visits and the total number from the start to fit your schedule.
Regret 2Skipping the Crown — "Tried to save money, ended up losing the tooth"
Why does this happen?A tooth that has had Root Canal Treatment becomes brittle, like a dry twig, because its moisture supply is cut off. If you use it without a Crown and it cracks down to the root (vertical fracture), there's no way to save it; it must be extracted and you'll need a Dental Implant. Trying to save the Crown cost ends up paying for an implant — the most costly false economy.
PreventionAfter a Root Canal Treatment, molars are only complete with a Crown. If the cost is a concern, don't delay—discuss material and cost options with your dentist. "Later" is the most expensive.
Regret 3"I had a Root Canal Treatment, but it hurts again" — reinfection and retreatment
Why does this happen?Root canals branch in complex ways in every person; rarely, residual nerve tissue or microcracks can cause ongoing pain or reinfection years later. If you leave it alone thinking "the treatment was done wrong," you may lose the tooth.
PreventionIf pain when biting lasts more than 2 weeks after treatment don't wait—come back for a follow-up. There is a critical window during which Root Canal Retreatment or Apicoectomy can save the tooth. The sooner we act, the higher the survival rate.
Regret 4"I was swayed by 'Let's just extract it and do a Dental Implant right away'"
Why does this happen?Sometimes a tooth that could be saved is first steered toward extraction and a Dental Implant with, "Even if you do a Root Canal Treatment, it won't last long." Once a natural tooth is extracted, that's it — no implant is as good as a natural tooth.
PreventionIf you've been advised to extract the tooth "Is there a chance to save it with Root Canal Treatment (or Root Canal Retreatment)?"Be sure to ask. Getting a second opinion on the chance of saving the tooth is your right as a patient.

02Root Canal Treatment side effects — how often they occur and what to do

Most side effects of Root Canal Treatment are temporary, and completing the treatment to the end is the best prevention.

Side effectsFrequency & patternWhat to do
Pain during/after treatment, discomfort when bitingCommon — a few days to 2 weeksTake prescribed medication; avoid chewing on that tooth. If it persists beyond 2 weeks, come in.
Acute worsening (flare-up)Occasionally — swelling and pain during treatmentManageable with medication and drainage. This is not a treatment failure—don’t panic; please return for care.
ReinfectionOccasionally — can occur even years laterAttempt to save the tooth with Root Canal Retreatment or Apicoectomy
Tooth discoloration (darkening)Occasionally — more noticeable in front teethCan be improved with non‑vital tooth whitening or with a restoration
Tooth fractureHigh risk if a Crown is not placedWith a vertical fracture, extraction is unavoidable. Prevention with a Crown is the only solution
Procedure-related issues, such as instrument separationRare — typically in cases with complex canal anatomyOften manageable at clinics that offer microscope-assisted root canal treatment
The frequencies and patterns noted are general trends and can vary with each person’s oral and overall health. For an accurate risk assessment, please speak with the clinical team.

03Who should reconsider

To be candid, Root Canal Treatment isn’t the right answer for everyone. If any of the following apply, discuss alternatives with your provider before rushing into treatment.

  • A tooth with a vertical root fracture — sadly, it cannot be saved with Root Canal Treatment. In this case, extraction followed by restoration is the appropriate choice.
  • A tooth with severe gum/bone destruction — the main problem may be periodontal rather than endodontic. Get a comprehensive evaluation of whether the tooth can be saved.
  • A schedule that won’t allow you to complete treatmentIf so — stopping a Root Canal Treatment midway can be worse than not having it at all. Agree on the total number of visits and schedule before you start.
  • 'A Dental Implant is better anyway' is all you’ve heard — and you’re about to choose extraction — first, assess whether the natural tooth can be saved. A Dental Implant is a replacement for a natural tooth, not an upgrade.

04Pre-treatment checklist — checking these can dramatically lower the chance of regret

Whichever dental clinic you visit is fine; if they clearly answer the items below, you can feel confident getting treatment there.

  • Treatment total number of visits and visit intervalshave been explained and you’ve secured the schedule
  • You understand that you must continue to the end even if the pain goes away
  • After treatment whether a Crown is needed and the costhave been confirmed in advance
  • You were advised to return if pain lasts more than 2 weeks after treatment
  • When extraction is recommended, you have confirmed the possibility of preservation (Root Canal Retreatment)
  • For complex cases, you have checked whether microscope-assisted root canal treatment is available

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.What happens if I stop a Root Canal Treatment?
Temporary fillings last only a few weeks. If bacteria re-enter the root canal during that time, it can become reinfected, requiring treatment to start over or, in severe cases, extraction. Even if your pain subsides, you must complete all planned appointments.
Q.After Root Canal Treatment, do I really need a crown?
For molars, it’s virtually essential. A tooth after Root Canal Treatment is dehydrated and weakened; if you chew without a crown and it splits down to the root, there may be no way to save it. If the crown cost feels heavy, don’t delay—talk with us about material options. Losing the tooth leads to a higher Dental Implant cost.
Q.I had Root Canal Treatment, but it hurts when I chew. Did it fail?
Discomfort for several days up to 2 weeks after treatment is part of normal healing. However, if it lasts beyond 2 weeks or keeps getting worse, there may be residual nerve tissue, micro-cracks, or reinfection—please come back for evaluation. Caught early, Root Canal Retreatment has a high chance of saving the tooth.
Q.How long can a tooth after Root Canal Treatment last?
If you finish with a crown and keep up with regular checkups, it can last for decades—often effectively for life. The three biggest lifespan reducers are skipping the crown, skipping checkups, and habits of chewing very hard foods.
Q.Wouldn’t it be better to extract it and get a Dental Implant?
If the tooth can be saved, Root Canal Treatment comes first. The sensation and function provided by the periodontal ligament of a natural tooth can’t be fully replicated by a Dental Implant. A Dental Implant is a second-best option when a tooth cannot be saved. If you’ve been advised to extract, consider a second opinion on whether it can be preserved.
