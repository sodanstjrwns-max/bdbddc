015 real reasons people regret their approach to Teeth Grinding (Bruxism)

Teeth Grinding (Bruxism) is the unconscious habit of grinding or clenching your teeth during sleep (or even during the day). You usually don’t notice it; a family member tells you, or your dentist spots the wear. Most regrets start with "Wearing a night guard will cure it"—that’s where it starts.

Regret 1"I thought just wearing it would fix it" — misunderstanding the role of a night guard
Why it happensA night guard (occlusal stabilization appliance) is not a device that stops teeth grinding. It acts as a protective shield, taking the grinding forces instead to protect your teeth and TMJ (Jaw Joint). If you expect it as a "treatment," a few months later you may be disappointed to hear, "I’m still grinding."
PreventionFrom the start, set your goal to "preventing damage," not "stopping grinding" and begin with that understanding. Then the appliance wearing down isn’t a failure, but evidence that it wore down instead of your teeth. This single shift in perspective can greatly improve satisfaction.
Regret 2"They told me it’s already worn down" — irreversible tooth wear
Why it happensThat’s why people search "teeth grinding tooth wear." Enamel does not regenerate. Chewing surfaces flatten and sensitivity develops; in severe cases, the teeth get shorter and even the proportions of the lower face can change. At that point, restoring with prosthetic work becomes major treatment.
PreventionThe only benefit is to stop it now. Photograph the wear to document it, then compare after 6 months~1 year to see whether progression has stopped. If sensitivity is significant, fluoride application and desensitizing toothpaste can help; for severe wear, consider prosthetic restoration.
Regret 3"It was uncomfortable, so I left it in a drawer" — discontinuing wear
Why it happensThe most common failure isn’t side effects, but not wearing it. Early on, the foreign-body sensation, extra saliva, and bulkiness make many people give up after just a few days. Then you’ve paid for it while your teeth keep wearing down.
PreventionIt usually takes 1~2 weeks to adapt. Starting with 30-minute daytime practice wear makes it much easier. If it hurts or presses on a spot, don’t just endure it—come in for an adjustment; it usually becomes comfortable after we adjust it. If the thickness or design isn’t a good match, we can switch to a different type.
Regret 4"I had Botox, but it came back" — expectations and repeat procedures
Why it happensThat’s why people search "bruxism Botox side effects." Injecting the masseter (chewing) muscles to reduce force can be effective, but it isn’t permanent and must be repeated periodically. If overdone, bite force can weaken or facial contours can change; rarely, you may feel side-to-side asymmetry.
PreventionBefore the procedure, "How long do the effects last, and when would I need it again? What about chewing strength?"See details here. Botox doesn’t replace a night guard but a complementary optionis the more accurate way to view it. It’s especially helpful when muscle pain is the main problem.
Regret 5"I didn’t address the causes" — stress, sleep, caffeine
Why it happensTeeth Grinding (Bruxism) is often related to stress, sleep quality, caffeine and alcohol, and sleep apnea. If you only wear a night guard and leave these factors unaddressed, the intensity won’t decrease. Years later, many people think, "I wish I’d had a sleep study back then."
Prevention tipsReduce caffeine and alcohol before bedtime and make sure you get enough sleep. If you snore loudly and feel heavy-headed in the morning, a sleep apnea testis recommended. Addressing the causes alongside it makes the night guard work better.

03Who should think twice

Frankly, a night guard isn’t the answer for everyone. If any of the following applies to you, talk with our clinicians about alternatives before rushing into treatment.

  • If you’re not sure you can wear the appliance every day — The most common failure isn’t side effects; it’s stopping use. If you don’t wear it, it won’t work.
  • Those expecting worn-down teeth to return to their original state — Enamel does not regenerate. The goal is to stop further progression.
  • Those expecting a night guard alone to stop teeth grinding (bruxism) — A night guard is a protective barrier, not a treatment that stops teeth grinding (bruxism).
  • Those who suspect snoring or sleep apnea but are delaying testing — Sleep issues and bruxism can be related. Testing for the underlying cause may need to come first.
  • Those whose main source of pain is a TMJ (jaw joint) disorder — A night guard alone may not be enough. You should also receive a TMJ evaluation.

04Pre-treatment checklist — Confirm these and your likelihood of regret drops dramatically

It doesn’t matter which dental clinic you choose. If the clinic can clearly answer the points below, you can feel confident receiving care there.

  • That a night guard is a protective barrier, not a treatment was explained
  • The current degree of tooth wear with photos was documented
  • Adjustment period (1~2 weeks) and how to adapt were explained
  • If it feels tight or hurts whether adjustments can be made was confirmed
  • The appliance’s replacement interval and cost were confirmed
  • If considering Botox, the duration of effect and the need for repeat sessions were explained
  • If you have snoring or sleep problems, a sleep apnea test was discussed as needed

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.Will wearing a night guard make my teeth grinding (bruxism) go away?
No. A night guard is not a treatment that stops teeth grinding (bruxism); it protects your teeth and TMJ from grinding forces as a protective barrier. There is no established method to completely eliminate bruxism itself. That’s why setting the goal as “damage prevention” is accurate; from that perspective, wear on the appliance is actually proof it’s doing its job.
Q.Can already worn teeth recover?
To be frank, enamel does not regenerate. The worn surfaces remain; if severe, sensitivity and reduced chewing function can occur and may require restoration. So stopping progression now is the only benefit. If you document your current status with photos, you can clearly compare later to see whether it has progressed.
Q.How long does a night guard last?
It varies by the intensity of your teeth grinding and how you care for it; if your bite force is strong, it will wear out faster. Replace the appliance if it wears down or cracks. This isn’t a malfunction but normal wear-and-tear. To avoid surprises later, confirm the replacement interval and cost before it’s made. At your routine checkups, have us check the appliance, too.
Q.Is Botox for bruxism safe?
It’s widely used, but it isn’t permanent, so repeat sessions are necessary. It helps a lot when muscle pain is the main issue. If the dose is too high, you may experience reduced chewing force, changes in facial contour, or, rarely, a sense of asymmetry. It’s not a replacement for a night guard—think of it as an option to use together. Be sure to confirm the duration of effect and the repeat interval in advance.
Q.How can I tell if I grind my teeth?
Common signs include ① a sore jaw or temple pain in the morning, ② flattened chewing surfaces, ③ sensitivity without an obvious cause, ④ composite resin or restorations that frequently chip or come off, ⑤ family members hear grinding sounds. In most cases, your dentist can confirm it by examining the wear patterns. We should also check for daytime clenching habits.
Q.Does teeth grinding affect the TMJ (Jaw Joint)?
It can. Repeated strong forces can strain the TMJ (Jaw Joint) and surrounding muscles, leading to pain, noises, and limited opening. Conversely, TMJ disorders can produce symptoms that look like bruxism. If you have noises or pain when opening, before making a night guard only a TMJ (Jaw Joint) evaluation is recommended as well.
