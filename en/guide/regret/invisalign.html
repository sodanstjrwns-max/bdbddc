The honest reality of clear aligners

If you start after only hearing "you can straighten your teeth without anyone noticing," you may regret it. Invisalign success depends not on the trays but on your commitment to wearing them 20~22 hours a day. Before you begin, here are the clear-aligner–specific regret points to consider.

Written by the Seoul BD Dental Medical Team As of 2026-06 About 8-minute read

30-second key summary

The #1 Invisalign regret is not meeting the 20~22 hours/day wear time, which lengthens treatment and throws off resultsIt’s not the trays—it’s a wear-time battle. Other common regrets: (1) attachments (small bumps bonded to teeth) show more than expected; (2) the hassle of removing them for every meal and brushing; (3) in complex cases, precision limits lead to additional finishing with braces. Most can be prevented by honestly assessing your daily routine and case suitability before you start.

015 real reasons people regret Invisalign

Invisalign is an excellent tool, but it isn’t a cure-all. Most regrets come from hearing only that it’s "clear and convenient" and not accounting for the cost of compliance.

Regret 1Wear-time failure — "I didn't realize I couldn't stick to it"
Why it happensWearing them 20~22 hours a day means you must keep them in at all times except for meals and brushing. If you have frequent socials, live with a coffee in hand, or tend to forget things, that's realistically hard. If wear time falls short, teeth won't move as planned—and both timeline and cost increase.
PreventionAsk yourself before you start — "Can I wear them at all times except for 2 hours a day?" If you're not confident, braces may actually be the less stressful choice. Accept from the outset that your cooperation determines the outcome—that's the first step.
Regret 2"I didn't think attachments would show this much"
Why it happensIn most cases, several small Composite Resin bumps (attachments) are bonded to the tooth surfaces. If you expected 'completely invisible,' you may be disappointed to see the little bumps up close.
PreventionIn my case, how many attachments will I need, and on which teeth will be placed—check in advance. Ask to see the attachment positions in the ClinCheck treatment simulation.
Regret 3The hassle of meals and brushing — "Even a cup of coffee becomes a chore"
Why it happensYou must remove the aligners for any food or drinks other than water, and brush before putting them back in. For people who snack or sip coffee while out, this can be more stressful than expected. If you drink with the aligners in out of convenience, it can lead to aligner staining and cavities.
PreventionReview your daily food and drink habits before you begin. Whether a portable toothbrush kit can become part of your everyday routineis a realistic way to judge fit.
Regret 4Timeline and extra trays — "Finished... but not finished"
Why it happensIt's not uncommon to finish all planned trays yet find your teeth haven't reached the planned positions, requiring refinement (additional trays). If you take this as a 'failure,' the disappointment grows.
PreventionRefinement is a normal part of clear aligner treatmentin practice. When you sign, confirm in writing how many rounds of additional trays are included and what triggers extra fees.
Regret 5Case limitations — "Braces would have been right from the start"
Why it happensSevere rotations, large vertical movements, and skeletal discrepancies can be hard to correct with clear aligners alone. If you push ahead with clear aligners and later have to switch to braces or add finishing braces, you'll pay in both extra time and cost.
Prevention"Is my case well-suited for Invisalign, or are braces more advantageous?"Ask this directly. An orthodontist who provides both options can compare the pros and cons impartially.

02Invisalign side effects overview — frequency and how to manage them

Most physical side effects of Invisalign are shared with braces (root resorption, gum recession, etc.). On top of that are issues unique to clear aligners.

Side EffectsFrequency and PatternWhat to Do
Tenderness when switching to a new trayVery common — usually adapts within 1–3 daysSwitch to a new tray before bedtime so you adapt while sleeping and feel more comfortable
Speech changes (slight lisp)Common — first 1–2 weeksMost people adapt naturally. Coordinate your start date around any important presentations
Aligner staining and odorCommon — depends on careDo not drink with them in; use a dedicated cleaner; always store them in the case
Tooth sensitivityOccasional — after IPR (interproximal reduction)Usually temporary. Visit the clinic if it persists
Root resorption or gum recessionCan occur, similar to bracesX-ray monitoring before and during treatment
Dry mouth or mouth ulcersRare — irritation from the aligner edgesCan be resolved by edge smoothing/adjustment; don’t just put up with it—come in
The frequencies and patterns are general trends and may vary with your oral condition and overall health. Please confirm your exact risk through a consultation with the clinical team.

03Who should think twice

Let’s be honest. Invisalign isn’t the right answer for everyone. If any of the following apply to you, discuss alternatives with the clinical team before starting treatment.

  • Not confident about wearing them 20–22 hours a day — the number one cause of Invisalign failure. If compliance is low, no clinician can guarantee results.
  • Frequent work dinners, coffee, or snacking — you’ll need to remove trays and brush several times a day. If you don’t, you may end up with cavities and staining.
  • Severe rotations or skeletal problems — clear aligners alone may have limitations. First get a comparison with braces or combination treatment.
  • Often misplace aligners — if a tray is lost, you’ll face remake costs and treatment delays. Wrapping them in a napkin and throwing them out is very common.
  • Expecting 'completely invisible' orthodontics — because of attachments and the aligner’s sheen, they are visible up close. Set realistic expectations to avoid disappointment.

04Pre-treatment checklist — Confirm these and you’ll greatly reduce the chance of regret

It doesn’t matter which dental clinic you choose. If they can clearly answer the points below, you can feel confident getting treatment there.

  • Whether wearing them 20–22 hours a day is possible in my life I have evaluated this honestly
  • In the ClinCheck simulation Attachments: number and positionsConfirmed.
  • Refinement (additional trays) included number and extra-fee policywere provided to me in writing
  • For my case, compared with braces pros and conswere explained
  • I confirmed the remake cost and turnaround time if a tray is lost
  • I was informed of the retention plan after treatment (essentially lifelong)
  • I confirmed whether an orthodontic specialist will be in charge of my treatment

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.What is the biggest reason people regret Invisalign?
Failure to meet wear time. If you don’t keep them in for 20~22 hours a day, your teeth won’t move as planned, treatment takes longer, and you’ll need additional trays. This isn’t a device problem but a compliance issue, so the most important step is to honestly assess your daily routine before you start.
Q.Is Invisalign really unnoticeable?
It’s true they’re much less visible than braces, but they’re not “completely invisible.” Because of the attachments (small composite bumps) on the teeth and the aligners’ sheen, they can be noticed at close range. It’s best to check in advance how many attachments you’ll need and where they’ll be placed, and set realistic expectations.
Q.I finished all the planned trays but my treatment isn’t done. Is that a failure?
No. Biological response to tooth movement varies by person, and finishing with additional trays (refinement) is common in clear aligner therapy. However, if you don’t confirm in the contract how many refinements are included and the basis for any extra fees, it can become a source of dispute.
Q.Can every case be treated with Invisalign?
No. It performs well for mild to moderate cases, but severe rotations, large vertical movements, or skeletal issues may be better handled with braces or combination approaches. Get a neutral comparison from an orthodontic specialist who works with both.
Q.Are Invisalign side effects different from those with braces?
Common orthodontic risks like root resorption and gum recession can occur with both. Clear aligners have some unique issues—temporary speech changes at the start, staining or odor of the aligners, and edge irritation from trays—most of which are manageable with care and adjustments.
Q.Can I drink coffee while wearing Invisalign?
We do not recommend any beverages other than water with the aligners in. Hot drinks can warp them, and colored drinks increase the risk of staining and cavities. Take them out to drink, and before putting them back in, brush (at least rinse with water).
