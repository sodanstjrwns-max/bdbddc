Scaling (Dental Cleaning) Side EffectsThe Truth,
"My teeth feel sensitive" is a misconception

"After Scaling (Dental Cleaning), my teeth feel sensitive," "the gaps between my teeth look wider" — common worries that make people postpone care. Bottom line: the culprit isn’t the cleaning; it’s the gum disease that tartar had been hiding. We’ll separate myths from facts.

Written by the Seoul BD Dental Medical Team As of June 2026 Approx. 6 min read

30-second key summary

Sensitivity or the feeling that teeth look more spaced after Scaling (Dental Cleaning) is not tooth damage; it’s the space tartar used to occupy now exposedIt’s normal for newly exposed root surfaces to feel sensitive for a short time, and for swollen gums to settle so spaces become more visible; both typically adapt within days to weeks. The real regret is delaying cleanings until it progresses to Periodontitis. Covered once per year by Korean NHIS (age 19+), so the cost burden is low.

014 real reasons people end up regretting Scaling (Dental Cleaning)

Regret about Scaling (Dental Cleaning) goes in the opposite direction from most treatments — it’s not "I regret doing it" but "I regret not doing it"in the vast majority of cases.

Regret 1"I put it off and ended up with Periodontitis" — regretting not doing it
Why it happensTartar (calculus) cannot be removed with a toothbrush. If left untreated, it can progress from gum inflammation (gingivitis) to periodontitis, where the supporting bone dissolves—and bone lost to periodontitis does not come back. If you delay for years because of the rumor that "it causes sensitivity" and end up needing gum surgery or an extraction, that is the real regret.
PreventionCovered once a year by Korean NHIS(age 19+) Use this benefit regularly. Scaling (Dental Cleaning) done when there is little tartar causes minimal sensitivity. The longer you put it off, the more uncomfortable it becomes.
Regret 2"My teeth felt sensitive after the cleaning" — regret caused by a misunderstanding
Why it happensWhen tartar has been covering exposed root surfaces and is removed, the previously hidden surface is revealed and can feel temporarily sensitive. Your tooth was not ground down. Without understanding this, it’s easy to think "Scaling (Dental Cleaning) ruined my teeth," and then avoid it—creating a vicious cycle.
PreventionSensitivity usually subsides within a few days to a few weeks. Use a desensitizing toothpaste, and if it persists, you can receive additional care such as fluoride application. Marked sensitivity is a sign that there was a lot of tartar and your gums were already inflamed.
Regret 3"The spaces between my teeth look wider"
Why it happensThe space once occupied by tartar and swollen gums becomes visible as your gums heal and settle after tartar removal. A new gap didn’t form; an existing one became visible — and again, this is not tooth damage.
PreventionThis space is the place for an interdental brush. In fact, now that a cleaning tool can fit there, switching to interdental brushes and floss will noticeably improve gum health.
Regret 4"Scaling (Dental Cleaning) alone wasn’t enough" — cases needing gum (periodontal) treatment
Why it happensIf periodontitis has already progressed, Scaling (Dental Cleaning) (removal of tartar above the gums) alone is not enough; root planing and subgingival curettage to remove tartar below the gums are needed. Some people feel reassured after a single scaling session, but their periodontitis keeps progressing.
PreventionAfter Scaling (Dental Cleaning) check your gum exam results (pocket depths)and ask whether additional periodontal treatment is needed. If bleeding or bad breath continues, it’s a sign that problems remain below the gums.

02Scaling (Dental Cleaning) side effects — frequency and how to manage them

Scaling (Dental Cleaning) is among the safest dental treatments. Most of the issues below are not "side effects of treatment" but "part of your gums’ healing process."

Side effectFrequency/PatternHow to manage
Temporary sensitivityCommon — if there was a lot of tartar; lasts a few days to a few weeksUse a desensitizing toothpaste; brush and rinse with lukewarm water. If it persists, fluoride application
Gum bleedingCommon — during or right after the procedureA normal response of previously inflamed gums. Stops within 1-2 days
Spaces between teeth become visibleCommon if heavy tartar was presentNot tooth damage. A sign to start using an interdental brush
Gum sorenessOccasionally — after removing deep tartarImproves within a few days. If severe, a pain reliever can help; if it persists, please visit us
Rough feeling on tooth surfacesOccasionallyResolved with polishing. A temporary change you may feel with your tongue
The frequencies and patterns above are general trends and may vary based on your oral and overall health. For an accurate risk assessment, please consult our clinicians.

03Please reconsider if this applies to you

We’ll be honest. Scaling (Dental Cleaning) isn’t the perfect answer for everyone. If any of the following apply to you, discuss alternatives with our clinicians before rushing into treatment.

  • In reality, very few people are truly unsuited for Scaling (Dental Cleaning). However, If you have a bleeding-tendency disorder or are taking anticoagulants,please be sure to tell us before the procedure.
  • If your gums are severely swollen from an acute gum infection, it may be more comfortable to first calm the inflammation with medication, then proceed step by step.
  • If you have a Dental Implant, — the area around implants must be maintained with dedicated instruments. Please let us know in advance whether you have implants.
  • If you require preventive antibiotics for heart disease or similar conditions, — we can treat you safely when you share your medical history.

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.Is it true that teeth get sensitive after Scaling (Dental Cleaning)?
Your teeth are not damaged. Tartar that had been covering an exposed root surface is removed, so the previously covered area becomes exposed and may feel sensitive temporarily. It usually settles within a few days to a few weeks, and sensitivity toothpaste and fluoride application can help.
Q.Does Scaling (Dental Cleaning) make the spaces between teeth wider?
No. As swollen gums heal and shrink, the space that tartar and swollen gum tissue had occupied becomes visible. A new gap did not form — an existing one that tartar had hidden is now revealed. Clean that space with an interdental brush.
Q.How often should I get Scaling (Dental Cleaning)?
In general, we recommend Scaling (Dental Cleaning) once every 6–12 months. If tartar builds up quickly, you smoke, or you have a history of periodontitis, every 3–6 months is better. For those aged 19 and older, one session per year is covered by Korean NHIS, reducing your out-of-pocket cost.
Q.Does Scaling (Dental Cleaning) wear down the tooth surface?
Scaling instruments work by breaking up and removing tartar (calculus). With proper technique, enamel is not meaningfully removed. The real risk is periodontitis from leaving tartar in place, which can lead to tooth loss.
Q.I’m bleeding after Scaling (Dental Cleaning) — is that okay?
If your gums are inflamed, some bleeding can occur during or right after the procedure and usually stops within 1–2 days. If bleeding continues or is heavy, please visit us. Healthy gums bleed very little even with scaling — the amount of bleeding is a good indicator of gum health.
