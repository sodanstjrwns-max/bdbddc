Wisdom Tooth Extraction Regretand Complications,
Regret from Delaying vs. Regret from a Bad Extraction

Regret around wisdom teeth comes from two directions: regret from putting it off — 'I should have taken it out sooner' — and regret from choosing the wrong place to have it removed — 'I went anywhere and suffered for it.' Either way, you can avoid it if you know ahead of time.

Written by the Seoul BD Dental Medical Team As of June 2026 Approx. 7 min read

30-second key summary

The two biggest sources of wisdom tooth regret are ① Letting it go until decay/infection spreads to the second molar, ② Removing an impacted tooth close to the nerve without a CT, causing altered sensationThat’s why, even without symptoms, get a panoramic X-ray at least once to check for impaction, and have nerve-proximate impactions removed at a clinic that can do a CT scan. Avoid straws, smoking, and vigorous brushing after extraction to prevent most cases of severe dry socket pain. Wisdom tooth extraction is covered by Korean NHIS, so the cost burden is modest.

01The 4 Real Reasons People Regret Wisdom Tooth Extraction

With wisdom teeth, it’s less about 'whether to remove' than 'when and where to remove them'is what determines whether you’ll regret it.

Regret 1"I should've had it removed earlier" — Neglect it, and you can lose the molar in front, too
Why it happensIf you leave an impacted Wisdom Tooth alone, a cavity can form between it and the second molar in front, and it may progress to gum inflammation or a cyst. In the end, because of one Wisdom Tooth, you could wind up needing Root Canal Treatment or even extraction of an otherwise healthy molar—the most regrettable outcome.
PreventionEven without symptoms get a panoramic X-ray at least once to check for impactionPlease do. If it’s a Wisdom Tooth that needs removal, the younger you are, the more flexible your bone is and the faster you recover. Taking it out in your 20s is very different in difficulty from taking it out in your 40s.
Regret 2Nerve injury — "My lip feels numb"
Why it happensLower Wisdom Teeth sit close to the inferior alveolar nerve, so a difficult extraction can cause temporary (rarely, permanent) altered sensation. Most injuries occur when someone attempts removal without a CT in cases where a panoramic X-ray alone cannot precisely determine the nerve’s distance.
PreventionIf the impacted tooth is close to the nerve a clinic with 3D CT imagingis where you should have it removed. Also check whether they can use nerve-sparing techniques such as crown sectioning or a two-stage extraction (coronectomy), if needed.
Regret 3Dry socket — "It hurt more than the extraction"
Why it happensIf the blood clot that seals the socket after extraction is dislodged, bone gets exposed and severe pain can last 3–7 days. Using straws, smoking, and vigorous rinsing are the main culprits; it happens when instructions aren’t heard or aren’t followed.
PreventionFor 24–48 hours after extraction avoid straws, smoking, vigorous brushing, and hot foods. Following just these four rules greatly reduces your dry socket risk. If the pain actually gets worse starting on day 3, come in immediately.
Regret 4"I wasted time going from clinic to clinic"
Why it happensChallenging impacted teeth are often referred from general dental offices to university hospitals. Add weeks of waiting for an appointment, and working patients burn vacation days—one extraction can end up taking a month.
PreventionFrom the start a clinic with extensive experience removing impacted Wisdom Teethcheck and make sure. Over the phone, you can ask, "Can you remove a horizontally impacted Wisdom Tooth in-house?"

02Wisdom Tooth extraction side effects — how common they are and what to do

Side effects from Wisdom Tooth extraction increase with difficulty (impaction depth and proximity to the nerve). Knowing the difficulty in advance is key to managing risk.

Side effectFrequency/PatternWhat to do
Pain, swelling, bruisingVery common — peaks at 2–3 days, then improvesTake prescribed medication and apply cold compresses for the first 48 hours. If it worsens beyond 1 week, come in.
Dry socketOccasional — risk increases with lower impacted Wisdom Teeth and in smokersWorsening pain around day 3 after extraction is a warning sign. Visit the clinic for a dressing to relieve it
Inferior alveolar nerve injury (numbness in the lip/jaw)Rare — impacted tooth close to the nerveUsually recovers within a few months, but can rarely persist long-term. CT evaluation helps prevent it
Lingual nerve injury (numbness of the tongue)Very rareIf it occurs, monitor the course and use medications. Inform the care team immediately
Persistent bleedingOccasional — caution if you’re on anticoagulantsGauze pressure for 30 minutes to 1 hour. Disclose all medications before extraction
Maxillary sinus perforation (upper Wisdom Tooth)Rare — when the root is close to the sinusUsually closes on its own. If water passes into your nose, come in immediately
The listed frequencies and patterns are general trends and can vary with your oral and overall health. Get an accurate risk assessment through a consultation with your clinician.

03Please think twice if this applies to you

To be frank, Wisdom Tooth extraction isn’t the right answer for everyone. If any of the below applies, discuss alternatives with the clinician before rushing into treatment.

  • Some Wisdom Teeth don’t need to be removed right away — If it erupts upright, can be cleaned well, has an opposing tooth to bite against, and shows no Cavity or inflammation, monitoring and maintenance are an option. Diagnosis comes first—not ‘automatic extraction.’
  • Severe acute inflammation present — In general, it’s safer to reduce the inflammation first, then extract. Choose a clinic that plans in stages rather than pushing for same-day, aggressive extraction.
  • Taking anticoagulants or osteoporosis medicationsIf so — you need management of bleeding risk and jawbone osteonecrosis. Disclose all medications and confirm whether co-management with internal medicine is needed.
  • PregnantIf so — timing should be adjusted unless it’s an emergency. The best scenario is to address Wisdom Teeth during a pre-pregnancy checkup.

04Pre-treatment checklist — confirming these can drastically cut your chances of regret

It doesn’t matter which dental clinic you choose. If the clinic can clearly answer the items below, you’re in good hands.

  • With a panoramic X-ray (CT if needed), the depth of impaction and the distance to the nervewere checked
  • For my Wisdom Tooth, the extraction difficulty and expected side effectswere explained
  • For nerve-proximity cases, CT evaluation and possible alternative techniques were confirmed
  • All current medications (e.g., anticoagulants) were disclosed
  • I received post-extraction instructions (how long to avoid straws, smoking, and brushing)
  • I confirmed whether extraction can be done in-clinic (including whether a university hospital referral is needed)

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.What happens if I don’t remove a Wisdom Tooth?
It depends on the case. A straight, well-maintained Wisdom Tooth can be kept, but leaving an impacted Wisdom Tooth alone can lead to a Cavity on the adjacent molar, gum inflammation, or a cyst. Even without symptoms, getting a panoramic X-ray at least once to check the status is a small investment that can prevent regret.
Q.How likely is nerve injury after a wisdom tooth extraction?
Overall it’s uncommon, but the risk increases with deeply impacted teeth whose roots contact the nerve. For nerve‑proximity cases, we confirm the distance with 3D CT and, if needed, extract at a clinic that can use nerve‑sparing techniques such as sectioning the tooth or a staged, two‑step extraction.
Q.What causes a dry socket, and how can I prevent it?
It occurs when the blood clot in the socket is dislodged and bone becomes exposed. For 24~48 hours after extraction, avoid straws, smoking, and forceful rinsing or brushing—these are key to prevention. If pain worsens starting on day 3, that’s a sign of dry socket; please come in.
Q.Can I have all four wisdom teeth removed at once?
It depends on your general health and case complexity. Because eating and recovery can be tougher, many patients split extractions left and right. If you’re considering sedation (conscious sedation), doing them all at once is also an option—discuss timing with your care team.
Q.Is wisdom tooth extraction covered by insurance?
Yes. Wisdom tooth extraction is covered by Korean NHIS; your out‑of‑pocket share varies by complexity (simple, complex, fully impacted) but is much lower than non‑covered (out‑of‑pocket) care. If you have private indemnity insurance, you may also submit an additional claim.
