01The 5 real reasons people regret Implant Overdentures

An Implant Overdenture uses 2–4 implants, and the denture snaps onto them. It holds better than a regular denture and costs less than doing the entire arch with implants. Why might someone end up regretting it? Most regret comes from a misunderstanding about the nameto begin with.

Regret 1"I thought I wouldn't need to take it out" — misunderstanding about the removable design
Why this happensBecause of the word “implant,” many people expect it to be fixed like natural teeth. But an Implant Overdenture is removable—you take it out and clean it every day. If you find this out after paying, it can be very disappointing.
How to prevent itDuring your consultation, "Will I be taking this out, or is it fixed?" Ask this clearly. There is a fully fixed option (fixed prosthesis), but it requires more implants and costs more. Trying a model or the real thing in your mouth before deciding helps prevent misunderstandings.
Regret 2"I have to replace the attachments?" — attachment replacement costs
Why this happensThe rubber or plastic parts of the attachment that hold the denture are consumables. They loosen with use and need periodic replacement. If you don’t know about this upkeep, you may find yourself thinking, “More money again.”
How to prevent it"How often do the attachments need replacement, and how much is it each time?" Check this in advance. It isn’t a defect; it’s planned maintenance by design. When calculating the total cost, don’t look only at the upfront fee—also consider the 5- and 10-year maintenance costs as well to get an accurate picture.
Regret 3"My gums hurt from pressure" — gum support and compression
Why this happensAn Implant Overdenture shares biting forces between the implants and the gums, so some areas of the gums are pressed. As the bone continues to change, it can stop fitting and become painful. Many people wait without getting adjustments and end up with sores.
How to prevent itIf it hurts, don’t tough it out— get an adjustment. In the first few weeks, needing several adjustments is normal. Over time, as your jawbone changes, you may need the inside relined (relining). It helps to ask in advance about the expected interval and cost.
Regret 4"I developed inflammation around the implant" — peri-implantitis
Why this happensThe implants that support the denture also need care. If you don’t take the denture out and clean around the implants, plaque builds up, causing peri-implantitis. In severe cases, you can lose the supporting implants, which can upend the entire denture plan.
How to prevent itEvery day, remove the denture and clean around the implants with a toothbrush and interdental brush. Store the denture separately after cleaning it. At 3–6 month checkups, have the gum tissue around the implants and the attachments checked together—this is the most reliable prevention.
Regret 5"I didn’t compare it with a regular denture" — not comparing your options
Why it happensThere’s a reason people search for “denture price,” “suction denture price,” and “complete denture price” together. Some patients do well with a well-made conventional denture, while others are much better served by an Implant Overdenture. If you’re offered only one option without comparison, you’re left wondering as much as the price difference.
Prevention"Among a conventional denture, an Implant Overdenture, and a fixed option, how does each compare for my jawbone condition? And what about the cost?"Ask that question. The lower jaw often doesn’t hold a conventional denture well, so an Implant Overdenture can make a big difference; the upper jaw tends to achieve better suction with a conventional denture. The answer varies with your specific condition.

02Implant Overdenture Side Effects — Frequency and What to Do

Side effects of Implant Overdentures fall into “rare but important to know” and “common but minor.” If you separate frequency from what to do, the vague anxiety tends to ease.

Side effectsFrequency & patternWhat to do
Gum pressure or sore spotsCommon — early adjustment periodUsually resolved with adjustments. Don’t tough it out—come in
Adjustment period for speech and chewingCommon — several weeksStart with soft foods and progress gradually. Practice reading aloud
Attachment looseningExpected wear — periodic replacementConfirm the replacement interval and cost in advance. If it’s loose, request an adjustment
Inside of denture no longer fits (bone changes)Common over timeAdjust with relining (refitting the inside)
Peri-implantitisWhen maintenance is lackingDaily cleanliness around implants + regular checkups every 3~6 months
Loss of a supporting implantRare — with inflammation or overloadTreatable if detected early. Regular checkups are key
The frequencies and patterns above are general trends and can vary with your oral condition and overall health. For an accurate risk assessment, please consult our clinicians.

03Please think twice if any of these apply to you

Candidly, an Implant Overdenture isn’t the right answer for everyone. If any of the following fit you, discuss alternatives with the clinical team before rushing into treatment.

  • Those who cannot commit to removing and cleaning it every day — An Implant Overdenture is removable. If maintenance is difficult, discuss a fixed option or other alternatives.
  • Those who cannot attend regular checkups — The attachments need periodic checks and care around the implants. Without maintenance, it can turn into regrets a few years later.
  • Those with uncontrolled diabetes or on long-term osteoporosis medications — There may be limitations to placing implants, so medical co-management/clearance comes first.
  • Heavy smokers without a plan to quit — Smoking greatly increases the risk of implant failure and peri-implantitis.
  • Those deciding based on upfront cost alone — There are ongoing maintenance costs for attachment replacement and relining. You should compare the total cost over 5~10 years.

04Pre-treatment checklist — Checking just these can greatly lower the chance of regret

It doesn’t matter which dental clinic you choose. If a clinic can clearly answer the points below, you can feel confident receiving care there.

  • Is it removable or fixed? clearly confirmed

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.Do I not need to remove an Implant Overdenture?
No. An Implant Overdenture is removable and must be taken out and cleaned dailyThe implants hold the denture, making it much more stable, but by design it is removable. If you want a non-removable option, consider a fixed prosthesis; the number of implants required and the cost will increase. Understand this difference first before deciding.
Q.How many implants are needed for an Implant Overdenture?
It depends on the case, but the lower jaw usually needs 2~4, and the upper jaw often needs more due to bone conditions. As the number increases, stability improves—and so does the cost. We can only decide after checking your jawbone with a CT, so be cautious of places that promise a set number and price without diagnostics.
Q.How much better is it than a conventional denture?
The biggest difference is loosenessIn particular, a lower full denture is hard to keep stable; creating anchor points with implants greatly improves chewing power and stability. However, there is still a tissue-bearing area that may need adjustments, and there are ongoing maintenance costs. For the upper jaw, suction with a conventional denture tends to be better, so the decision depends on your conditions.
Q.How often do the attachment components need to be replaced?
It depends on your habits and the type of attachment. If you bite forcefully or remove and insert it often, it will loosen sooner. The key is that this is expected wear-and-tear, not a malfunction. Before signing, confirm the replacement interval and per-visit cost, include them in the total cost, and you won’t be caught off guard later.
Q.I’m 65 or older—am I covered by Korean NHIS?
For patients 65 and older, certain denture and implant items are covered by Korean NHIS. However, there are defined criteria (whether you are fully edentulous, limits on number, remake intervals, etc.), and whether they apply as-is to an Implant Overdenture must be confirmed case by case. During your consultation, get the covered items and your out-of-pocket amount in writing is the most accurate way.
Q.How do I care for an Implant Overdenture?
① Remove your denture each day and clean it with a dedicated cleanser, ② with the denture out brush around the implants with a toothbrush and an interdental brush separately, ③ At night, you usually remove it and store it in water. ④ At regular checkups every 3~6 months, we check the attachment components and the gums around the implants. The most common mistake is skipping cleaning around the implants.
