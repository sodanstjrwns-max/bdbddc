015 real reasons people regret their retainers

A retainer holds teeth moved by orthodontic treatment in place until they settle, and afterward keeps them from relapsing. The regret isn’t about the retainer itself but the moment you think "I'm done now"is when it begins.

Regret 1"I thought I was done and stopped wearing it" — Stopping wear and relapse
Why it happensThe first few years after removing your braces or aligners carry the highest risk of relapse. Rotations of the front teeth and gaps between teeth tend to come back. A large share of searches like "regretting braces" or "regretting clear aligners" are actually about this relapse. Few things are more discouraging than seeing years of effort and expense undone.
PreventionA retainer is the start of lifelong maintenanceUnderstand it that way. At first, wear it most of the day; later, reduce to nights only, but it’s safest not to stop entirely. Night-only wear is easier than you might think and far more economical than getting orthodontic treatment again.
Regret 2"I lost it, and months have passed" — Neglect after loss
Why it happensThe loss itself isn’t the problem; how long you leave it unaddressedis. A few days is fine, but after a few weeks your teeth may have already shifted and the old retainer won’t fit, and to make a new one we must take impressions again. Wait longer, and partial retreatment may be needed.
PreventionIf you lose it, contact the clinic within a few days. Remaking a retainer costs far less than retreatment. You can also have a spare made. If it went missing during travel or a move, don’t put it off.
Regret 3"I didn't realize the fixed retainer had come off" — Fixed retainer debonding
Why it happensA fixed retainer bonded to the inside of the front teeth is convenient, but many people don’t notice when the bonding has come loose. If only one side is detached for months, that segment can move and the alignment can actually worsen.
PreventionWith your tongue, occasionally sweep along the inside wire to check itand make a habit of it. If you feel it has come loose, come in right away. Tartar tends to build up around fixed retainers, so use a floss threader or an interdental brush to clean there, and have the bonding checked at regular checkups.
Regret 4"No one told me I'd need to wear it for life" — Insufficient explanation of wear duration
Why it happensIf you finish believing "I only need to wear it for six months," then later hear "It's better to keep wearing it at night," you may feel you weren’t adequately informed. Misunderstanding the wear duration is the biggest cause of relapse.
PreventionBefore starting orthodontic treatment, at the agreement stage, "How long do I need to wear the retainer, and how many hours per day? Is the cost of remaking it included?"Confirm this. When comparing total costs, check whether the retainer and any remake fees are included for an accurate comparison.
Regret 5"The retainer smells and is discolored" — Inadequate hygiene care
Why it happensClear retainers can warp in hot water, and if you don’t clean them, they develop odor and discoloration. That makes you reluctant to wear them and, eventually, you stop. Hygiene problems are a common path to relapse (teeth shifting back).
PreventionCold or lukewarm waterClean only with cold or lukewarm water, and use a dedicated retainer cleanser. Hot water and dishwashers can deform it, so avoid them. Brush your teeth before putting it in, and store it in a ventilated case. Without odor or discoloration, wearing it feels much easier.

02Retainer side effects — how common and how to manage them

Retainer side effects fall into “rare but important” and “common but mild.” Knowing how often they occur and what to do about them reduces needless worry.

03Please reconsider if any of these apply to you

Frankly, a retainer isn’t the right answer for everyone. If any of the following fit you, discuss alternatives with our clinicians before starting treatment.

  • If you don’t think you can wear the retainer at night — you need to accept the risk of relapse before starting. There’s no way to keep results without retention.
  • If you tend to put off remaking it after losing it — if the gap goes on too long, remaking alone won’t solve it; limited orthodontic retreatment may be needed.
  • If you find hygiene around a fixed retainer difficult — calculus buildup can lead to gum problems. Consider a removable option as well.
  • If you expect that “once orthodontic treatment ends, the care ends” — retention is the start of lifelong maintenance. Please set your expectations first.
  • If you don’t plan to come for regular checkups after orthodontics — fixed retainer debonding and relapse are caught early at checkups.

05Frequently Asked Questions

Q.How long do I need to wear my retainer?
Practically speaking, as long as possibleis the correct answer. The first few years after your braces come off carry the highest risk of relapse, and even after that teeth continue to move slightly. So the common recommendation is to wear it most of the day at first, then reduce to nights only—without stopping entirely. The belief that "only 6 months" is enough is one of the most common causes of relapse.
Q.I lost my retainer—what should I do?
within a few days contact the clinic. If it’s only been a short time, a remake will solve it. After more than a few weeks, your teeth may have shifted and new impressions are needed; after a few months, partial re-orthodontic treatment may be required. Remaking a retainer costs far less than redoing orthodontic treatment, so acting promptly is the most economical choice.
Q.Which is better, fixed or removable?
Each has pros and cons. Fixed (a wire behind the front teeth) means you can’t forget to wear it, but tartar can build up and it may come off without you noticing. Removable (e.g., a clear tray) is easy to clean but only works if you wear it as directed. It’s also common to use both togetheris common. We decide after considering your tendency for relapse in the front teeth and your oral hygiene habits.
Q.If I don’t wear my retainer, will my teeth really shift back?
They are likely to shift back. In particular, rotated front teeth, gaps between teeth, and areas that were moved a lot tend to relapse. There is individual variation—some people see little change for years—but there’s no way to know in advance, so "testing" whether it’s okay not to wear it is a risky gamble. The way to protect years of effort and expense is simply to wear your retainer at night.
Q.It hurts when I put it back on after not wearing it for a long time—should I keep wearing it?
If it feels tight, that’s a sign your teeth have already moved a bit. Forcing it can crack the appliance or put excessive force on your teeth, so if it doesn’t seat well, don’t force it—have the clinic check it.
Q.How do I clean a clear retainer?
With cold or lukewarm watergently clean it and use a dedicated cleaner. Hot water, boiling water, and dishwashers can directly cause warping, so avoid them completely. Scrubbing hard with toothpaste will cloud the surface. Store it in a ventilated case, and before wearing it, brush your teeth to greatly reduce odor and discoloration.
