Consultation

Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan limited fixed-brace treatmentCould a limited plan address your concern without doing more than you need?

You may want several front teeth improved but feel uncertain about committing to comprehensive treatment. “Light” can sound less invasive or less demanding, yet the daily appliance care is still real. A useful limited plan explains both the changes it aims for and the concerns it deliberately does not address.

That must be specified in your individual plan after the bite is assessed.

03

Can I add more changes later?

Possibly, but this may require a new plan, more time and additional cost.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

A defined limited plan can focus on selected alignment concerns.

What to weigh up

Some bite or wider alignment issues may be left unchanged.

The question that helps you choose

Accept a limited objective only when its remaining compromises are clear.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Draw the boundary of the treatment

Assess the full bite before defining a limited area. Space, tooth angulation, roots and gum support can affect whether the proposed improvement is appropriate. Ask which teeth will move and whether the opposite arch needs attention. A shorter-looking list of teeth does not automatically make the plan simpler.

02

Compare a compromise with a comprehensive goal

A limited plan may be reasonable when the goal is specific and remaining relationships are acceptable. It may not address a deeper bite or broader alignment issue. You should understand those tradeoffs in ordinary language, including whether another option would better match the discomfort that brought you to consultation.

03

Plan adjustments, repair and retention

Fixed braces require cleaning around brackets and scheduled checks even when treatment is limited. Food choices, breakages and work commitments affect how practical the plan is. Ask how completion is judged and what would trigger a change in scope. Retainers and their future replacement should be discussed at the beginning.

Potential benefits

Limitations and risks

Limiting scope may not be suitable when the concern depends on the whole bite.

Decay, gum problems, root changes and relapse remain possible; the package name does not remove orthodontic risks.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Clean carefully around brackets and keep ordinary dental reviews during treatment.

Report breakages and follow the agreed adjustment and long-term retention plan.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is limited treatment always faster?

No. The difficulty of the required movement can matter as much as the number of teeth.

Can braces on one arch affect the bite?

Changes in one arch need to be considered against the opposing teeth; ask how this will be monitored.

Will I need space between teeth to be created?

Space requirements and possible methods must be assessed and explained rather than assumed from the package.

How do we know when it is finished?

Agree on alignment, bite and maintenance criteria, not only a target calendar date.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • What is outside the scope of this plan?
  • Why is the remaining compromise acceptable?
  • How will the final bite and retention be checked?
