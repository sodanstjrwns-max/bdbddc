Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan dental implantsHas one missing tooth changed the way you eat?

You may have become used to chewing on one side or hiding a gap when you smile. Surgery, healing and an unfamiliar estimate can feel more difficult than living with the missing tooth. Start with what you want to do comfortably again. You do not have to choose an implant before understanding the alternatives.

You deserve to understand what this means for your life.

Do I have to have an implant?

No. Tooth preservation, bridges and dentures may be alternatives, depending on the examination and your priorities.

02

Could it fail later?

Healing problems, surrounding inflammation and component problems are different risks. Discuss how each would be recognised and managed.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

An implant can support a replacement without using adjacent teeth as bridge supports.

What to weigh up

Surgery, healing and lifelong cleaning and reviews remain necessary.

The question that helps you choose

Compare bone and gum conditions, alternative treatments and the care you can maintain.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

First decide whether a tooth can be saved

If a tooth is still present, its remaining structure, roots and supporting tissues need assessment before extraction is agreed. Saving it and replacing it are different decisions. For an existing gap, compare an implant with a bridge or removable denture. The condition of neighbouring teeth and your preference about surgery matter as much as the gap itself.

02

Understand the complete treatment

An implant restoration includes a body in the bone, connecting components and the tooth you chew with. Assessment, any preparatory treatment, surgery, healing and the final restoration are separate stages. Ask what you will wear and eat between them. A temporary tooth does not necessarily mean unrestricted chewing or completion of treatment.

03

Make maintenance part of the design

A replacement should be practical to clean and review, not merely look complete in a photograph. Discuss gum health, smoking, medical conditions and prescribed medicines openly. These are planning information, not reasons for blame. If travel to Cheonan is difficult, arrange realistic review visits and a contact plan for unexpected discomfort.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

A fixed replacement may make eating and speaking more comfortable.

The design can avoid preparing healthy neighbouring teeth for a conventional bridge.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Infection, failure to integrate and injury to nearby structures are possible surgical risks.

Gum inflammation, loose screws or restoration fractures may need further care.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Bring a medication list and any previous imaging or implant records.

Report new looseness, pus or worsening swelling rather than testing the tooth with hard food.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is age alone a reason I cannot have an implant?

Suitability depends on health, growth where relevant, local tissues and maintenance ability. An individual assessment is more useful than age alone.

Does a better brand remove the need for grafting?

No. Product choice does not replace assessment of the available bone or the planned tooth position.

Can I eat normally on the day of surgery?

Only follow the instructions for your particular temporary restoration and surgical conditions. Early loading is a separate decision.

Will it last for life?

No individual lifespan can be promised. Healing, hygiene, gum disease and mechanical stresses affect ongoing care needs.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Can the existing tooth still be preserved?
  • What will I use while the area heals?
  • Which treatment and maintenance items are included?
