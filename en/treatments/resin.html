Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan composite dental fillingsIs a filling enough to rebuild this tooth safely?

A small chip, a new cavity or an old filling that keeps breaking can leave you uncertain about how much treatment is really needed. A tooth-coloured filling may feel like the simplest choice. Its suitability depends on the remaining tooth, the forces it receives and whether a sound, cleanable restoration can be made.

01 / 04Assess even small defects

A schematic guide to structures and decisions, not an individual result or treatment timeline.

  1. 01

    Assess even small defects

    Consider remaining tooth and bite forces as well as the chip size. Concentrated force can change the choice for a small defect.

  2. 02

    Filling or coverage

    Compare direct composite with a restoration that protects a cusp. Material strength and the strength of the remaining tooth are different considerations.

  3. 03

    Shape and contacts

    Restore the required area and check shape and contacts. When closing a space, preserve a form that can be cleaned.

  4. 04

    Consider repair

    Assess the extent of staining, wear or chipping. Discuss whether repair or complete replacement is appropriate.

Bonding conditions, wear and force can limit durability.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Match the restoration to the remaining tooth

Composite is placed and shaped directly to replace selected missing tooth structure. The size and location of the defect, the remaining walls and cusps and the bite affect suitability. A visibly small corner under heavy force may behave differently from a larger protected area. Ask why a filling is adequate or why additional coverage is recommended.

02

Understand bonding and shape

The conditions for bonding, the contact with the neighbouring tooth and the restoration margin matter. The goal is not only a matching colour but a surface that functions and can be cleaned. If food regularly traps around the old filling, ask whether its shape or contact needs correction as well as replacement material.

03

Plan for wear, repair and symptoms

Composite can stain, wear or fracture. Some defects may be repaired while others require replacement or a different type of restoration. Discuss persistent sensitivity or biting discomfort after treatment. Repeatedly replacing the same broken filling without assessing the remaining tooth and forces may leave the cause unresolved.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Shape and colour can be addressed directly in suitable cases.

Localised defects may sometimes be repaired rather than replacing everything.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Staining, wear, fracture or recurrent decay may require further care.

A large filling may not provide the protection needed for weakened cusps.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Keep the restoration margins and neighbouring contact clean.

Seek review for a high bite, persistent symptoms or repeated food trapping.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is tooth-coloured material always best?

Material choice depends on the site, size, bonding and bite. No material is ideal for every defect.

Does a filling mean no tooth preparation?

Decay or unsuitable old material may need removal; ask what is proposed and why.

Can composite close a front gap?

Sometimes, but tooth proportions, the cause of the gap and cleaning access need assessment.

Will whitening change the filling’s colour?

No. Coordinate shade planning because composite and natural teeth respond differently.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Is there enough sound tooth for a direct filling?
  • Does a cusp need additional protection?
  • What caused the previous filling to fail?
