03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Separate material, surface and implant dimensions

Roxolid and SLActive refer to different aspects of a product system. Neither name describes the whole restoration or your available bone. Ask which actual model and dimensions are proposed, why they fit the site and whether the connection and final restoration meet your needs. Avoid assuming that one label resolves every anatomical limitation.

02

Compare the clinical alternatives

A proposed implant should be compared with other appropriate systems and, where relevant, bridges or dentures. If a different dimension is being considered, ask how the supporting bone and planned forces are assessed. The product name alone does not establish that grafting, an incision or a healing interval can be avoided.

03

Include long-term service in the decision

Discuss the connecting parts, crown design and future repair arrangements. Keep the exact system information, particularly if you may move or receive care elsewhere. A useful estimate distinguishes the implant body from the entire treatment, and any stated coverage should be read as specific terms rather than a lifetime clinical guarantee.

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Does this mean a smaller implant is suitable for me?

Dimensions must be selected for the available tissues and planned forces. Suitability cannot be inferred from material alone.

Can I avoid grafting with this system?

That depends on your site and restoration plan; the brand does not answer it.

Is the most expensive option always best?

Compare expected benefits, alternatives, included stages and practical maintenance rather than price as a proxy for outcome.

What if a component loosens?

Have it assessed. Avoid trying to tighten or glue it yourself; the problem may be limited to a component.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Which exact model, surface and dimensions are proposed?
  • How does this choice change my treatment compared with alternatives?
  • What records and service provisions are included?
