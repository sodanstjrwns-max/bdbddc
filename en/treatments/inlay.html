Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan dental inlays and onlaysWhy is a simple filling no longer being recommended?

You may expect a replacement filling and feel unsettled when an inlay, onlay or crown is suggested instead. The names can sound like different price levels rather than different ways to support the tooth. The useful question is which parts of your tooth remain strong and which need protection.

01 / The questions behind the treatment name

You deserve to understand what this means for your life.

01

What is the difference between an inlay and an onlay?

An onlay covers vulnerable cusps; an inlay mainly restores within the tooth’s existing outline.

02

Why not use another direct filling?

The remaining structure and bite may require a different restoration, but the reason should be explained.

03

Can the plan change after the old filling is removed?

Yes. Newly visible damage may affect the required protection.

What may improve

A selected coverage design can restore damage while retaining suitable tooth structure.

What to weigh up

Preparation, bonding and later repair needs depend on the defect and material.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Understand the coverage difference

An inlay fits within a defect, while an onlay covers one or more cusps when that protection is needed. A crown covers more of the tooth. The appropriate boundary depends on the damage, cracks, remaining structure and bite. Ask to see which area needs support rather than choosing from the restoration name alone.

02

Compare materials and bonding conditions

Different materials have different handling, appearance and maintenance considerations. The location of the margin, moisture control and forces also matter. No material is automatically superior in every tooth. Discuss the rationale for the proposed material and whether a direct filling or another coverage design is a suitable alternative.

03

Allow for findings during preparation

Removing an old restoration can reveal damage that was not fully visible at the first examination. This may change the required coverage. Ask how such changes will be discussed before more treatment proceeds. Temporary protection, the final fit and review of the bite are also parts of the treatment plan.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

An onlay can protect selected cusps where appropriate.

Restoration boundaries can be tailored to the remaining tooth and defect.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Fracture, debonding, sensitivity and recurrent decay are possible.

An inlay alone may not protect a weakened or cracked cusp adequately.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Follow instructions for any temporary restoration and report loosening.

Have a high bite, lasting sensitivity or food trapping checked after placement.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is ceramic always better than another material?

No. The site, remaining tooth, bonding and bite should guide the choice.

Does an onlay always avoid a crown later?

No future treatment can be ruled out; the goal is suitable protection for the current condition.

Why might a crown become necessary?

The extent or position of newly identified damage may require broader coverage.

How do I compare estimates?

Check the proposed coverage, material, temporary stage and final fit rather than comparing names alone.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Which cusps or walls need protection?
  • What alternative coverage is possible?
  • What findings could change the plan?
