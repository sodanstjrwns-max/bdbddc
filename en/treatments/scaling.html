Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan dental scalingDo sensitive teeth or bleeding gums make cleaning appointments difficult?

You may avoid scaling because you remember sharp sensitivity, or worry that removing calculus will loosen your teeth. Bleeding during brushing can make you afraid to clean at all. A useful appointment explains what is being removed, checks why the gums bleed and adapts the approach to the areas that are uncomfortable.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

Removing plaque-retaining deposits supports gum care and assessment.

What to weigh up

Sensitivity may occur and deeper disease may need additional treatment.

The question that helps you choose

Match the cleaning scope and review plan to the actual gum findings.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Separate surface cleaning from deeper gum treatment

Scaling removes deposits that ordinary brushing cannot remove. It is not the same as every form of periodontal treatment. Gum measurements, examination and, where needed, imaging help identify deeper disease. Ask whether routine scaling is sufficient or whether root-surface treatment and reassessment are needed.

02

Understand sensitivity and visible spaces

Exposed roots, inflammation, decay and other conditions can contribute to sensitivity. Tell the team where it occurs and how long it lasts. Removing deposits or reducing swelling can reveal spaces that were previously less obvious; that does not automatically mean the cleaning damaged otherwise healthy support.

03

Continue safe cleaning between visits

Avoiding brushing because of bleeding can make plaque control harder. Ask for a suitable technique and tools rather than forcing painful areas. Medical conditions and medicines may affect planning; provide accurate information and do not stop prescriptions yourself. Review frequency should reflect your gum condition and risk, not a universal calendar rule.

Potential benefits

Limitations and risks

Scaling alone cannot restore all lost periodontal support.

Ongoing plaque accumulation or untreated risk factors can lead to recurrence.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Explain previous sensitivity and bring your medication information.

Continue the recommended home-care routine and have prolonged or worsening symptoms checked.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is bleeding always caused by brushing too hard?

No. Inflammation and other factors need assessment; technique should be checked as well.

Do I need antibiotics before scaling?

Only selected circumstances require preventive medication. Discuss relevant medical history rather than taking antibiotics automatically.

Should I stop blood-thinning medicine?

Do not stop it yourself. The dental team can assess the procedure and coordinate advice if needed.

Why are gaps more visible afterwards?

Reduced swelling or removal of deposits can reveal existing contours; the gum and bone condition should be explained.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Is this routine cleaning or treatment for deeper disease?
  • How can sensitivity be managed during care?
  • Which areas need a different home-cleaning method?
