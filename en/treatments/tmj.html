Specialized TMJ (Jaw Joint) Disorder Treatment

TMJ DisorderWhat is it?

TMJ disorder (TMD) is a condition in which pain, noises, or limited movement occur in the jaw joint or surrounding muscles. Teeth Grinding (Bruxism), stress, Malocclusion can be causes; treatment may include a splint, physical therapy, or Botox.

TMJ disorder (TMD) is a collective term for conditions causing pain, noise, or limited opening in the jaw joint and surrounding muscles. Typical signs include “clicking” or “creaking” when you open your mouth, jaw pain, or difficulty opening wide.

If there’s only noise without pain, treatment isn’t always necessary. However, if pain occurs, your mouth won’t open well, or the sound worsens, you should be evaluated by a specialist. Leaving it untreated can make it worse.

Teeth grinding/clenching habits, stress, trauma, malocclusion, chewing only on one side, and prolonged mouth opening (dental treatment, yawning) can all contribute. Multiple factors often act together.

① Occlusal stabilization splint(splint) wear ② Medication (anti-inflammatory pain relievers, muscle relaxants) ③ Physical therapy ④ Habit correction (avoid clenching) ⑤ Botox injections (for severe muscle tightness). Most cases improve with conservative care, and surgery is rarely needed.

It’s hard to eliminate grinding completely, but a nighttime occlusal guard (night guard) can reduce tooth wear and TMJ load. Stress management, cutting back on caffeine, and relaxation exercises before bed also help.

Mild cases: 2–4 weeks; moderate: 3–6 months; chronic TMJ disorder: 6 months or more. Consistent treatment and habit changes are important.

Recognize + correct clenching habits, reduce hard foods (squid, ice, etc.), chew evenly on both sides, avoid resting your chin on your hand, manage stress, and get enough sleep.
