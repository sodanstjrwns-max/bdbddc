Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan cosmetic composite bondingCould a small change help without covering the whole tooth?

A chipped corner or small gap can become the only thing you see in a photograph. You may want it improved while feeling reluctant to commit to veneers or extensive preparation. Bonding can be worth discussing, but the right amount of change depends on tooth proportions, bite and the reason the space or damage appeared.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Understand what composite can change

Tooth-coloured composite can be added or shaped to address selected defects and proportions. It does not move the root or correct every alignment problem. If position, widespread colour or significant damage is the concern, compare orthodontics, whitening or another restoration rather than assuming one material can solve everything.

02

Keep the shape practical as well as attractive

Closing a gap by adding material changes tooth width and the cleaning space. Repairing an edge places material where biting forces may act. Ask how the proposed contour relates to the gums and bite, and whether it can be kept clean. A small visual target can still need careful functional planning.

03

Expect maintenance rather than permanence

Composite may stain, wear or chip and can sometimes be repaired. The appropriate response depends on the defect and surrounding tooth. Discuss polishing, repair and replacement before treatment, including what happens if your goals later change. A natural appearance also depends on shade, surface and the condition of neighbouring teeth.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

A localised repair may preserve more tooth structure than a larger covering restoration when appropriate.

Some composite defects can be adjusted or repaired without replacing the whole restoration.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Bonding does not correct every tooth-position or bite issue.

Edges under heavy force may chip, and an unsuitable contour can complicate cleaning.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Avoid using restored edges to open packaging or bite hard objects.

Maintain cleaning and review any roughness, chipping or changes at the margin.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

How is this different from a veneer?

A veneer is a covering restoration; direct bonding uses shaped composite. Their preparation, appearance and maintenance differ.

Can bonding replace orthodontics?

It changes shape, not actual tooth position. Discuss whether the desired look would create an unsuitable contour.

Can I whiten bonded teeth?

Composite and natural teeth do not change colour together. Coordinate whitening and bonding plans.

What if a small piece chips?

Seek assessment rather than smoothing or gluing it yourself. Repair may be possible depending on the defect.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • What caused the gap or damaged edge?
  • Will the proposed shape remain easy to clean?
  • What maintenance and repairs should I expect?
