Consultation

Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan holiday implant appointmentsIs taking time off harder than arranging the treatment itself?

Work, childcare or a long journey may leave a public holiday as your only realistic opportunity to visit. You should be able to explain that constraint without feeling rushed into surgery. A holiday is an appointment option, not a different implant technique, and it does not make assessment or healing stages disappear.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

A confirmed holiday appointment may reduce conflict with work or family commitments.

What to weigh up

Later stages and unexpected reviews may still require additional travel or time off.

The question that helps you choose

Choose a schedule that supports assessment and recovery, rather than compressing necessary treatment.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Confirm what is available on the particular date

Ask which clinician is seeing patients and whether the appointment can include an examination, imaging, follow-up or a planned procedure. Availability must be confirmed for the actual date. An initial consultation is not a promise that implant surgery will be suitable or performed that day.

02

Prepare information so the visit is useful

Bring a medication list, relevant medical history and previous dental records if available. Explain whether your main concern is a missing tooth, a failing implant or uncertainty about an extraction recommendation. Clear priorities help the assessment focus on the decisions you need, even if treatment must happen later.

03

Include recovery and follow-up in the calendar

A holiday appointment can help with access, but surgery may still affect eating and work afterwards. Healing checks, restoration visits and unexpected reviews may fall on ordinary days. Discuss those possibilities before committing. A temporary tooth and the final tooth are separate stages, regardless of the day surgery takes place.

Potential benefits

Appointment planning can make an initial assessment more accessible.

Limitations and risks

Holiday availability does not establish clinical suitability for surgery.

Treatment stages cannot always be combined to match a preferred schedule.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Confirm the appointment and preparation instructions directly before travelling.

Ask how to obtain help if postoperative symptoms worsen outside the planned review date.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is holiday implant surgery medically different?

No. The same assessment, consent, surgical and follow-up considerations apply.

Should I fast just in case?

Follow the clinic’s instructions for the planned appointment. Do not guess preparation requirements from the possibility of surgery.

Can I return to work the next day?

Discuss your duties and the expected procedure. Recovery needs vary and should not be promised from the calendar alone.

What if I can only return months later?

Explain this before treatment. The required reviews and restoration stages need a practical arrangement.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • What can this specific appointment include?
  • Which later visits are likely to be necessary?
  • What is the contact plan if symptoms change?
