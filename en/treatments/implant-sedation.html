Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan implant treatment with conscious sedationIs fear of the procedure stopping you from replacing a tooth?

You may understand why an implant is needed yet feel your body tense at the thought of the chair, injections or drilling. A previous difficult visit can make “just relax” sound unhelpful. Sedation may be one option, but fear of losing control or not waking normally deserves an explanation too.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Sedation and pain control have different jobs

Conscious sedation aims to reduce anxiety while maintaining a level of responsiveness; it is not the same as general anaesthesia. Awareness and recall vary. Local anaesthetic is still used to control surgical pain. Before treatment, agree how you can signal discomfort, when the team would pause and which sensations may still be noticed.

02

Assess health before choosing the method

Discuss all medicines, allergies, previous sedation experiences, breathing problems and sleep apnoea. Sedation can affect breathing and circulation, so assessment, monitoring and recovery arrangements are essential. Do not stop prescribed medicines or take extra sedatives yourself. Preparation instructions depend on the selected method and must come from the treating team.

03

Plan the whole day, not only the operation

Ask about accompaniment, transport, eating instructions, recovery and restrictions on driving or important decisions. Feeling awake does not replace discharge guidance. Implant healing and chewing restrictions still apply after sedation wears off. A slower visit, breaks, explanation and local anaesthetic without sedation may also be appropriate options.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Give an accurate medication list, including supplements and any medicines taken for sleep or anxiety.

Arrange the escort and transport required for your method before the appointment.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Does sedation make implant healing faster?

It addresses anxiety during care. Bone healing, surgical suitability and the timing of the restoration require their own assessment.

Can I drive if I feel normal afterwards?

Follow the specific discharge restrictions. Feeling normal is not a reliable substitute for the required recovery period.

Will I need to fast?

Instructions differ by method and individual circumstances. Obtain written preparation guidance rather than following a generic internet schedule.

Can we try treatment without sedation first?

Where appropriate, discuss shorter visits, breaks and gradual preparation. The decision should reflect the procedure and your needs.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Which sedation method and depth are being proposed?
  • How will discomfort or a breathing problem be managed?
  • What escort and recovery arrangements do I need?
