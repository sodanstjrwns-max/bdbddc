Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan invisalign moderateDo several tooth movements need to work together?

You may see crowding or gaps in several places and wonder why a small aligner plan is not enough. At the same time, a comprehensive plan may feel larger than you expected. Moderate should describe a clinically assessed scope, with an explanation of the movements and daily commitments, rather than simply a price between two other choices.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Understand how the movements interact

Creating space, rotating teeth and coordinating the bite can involve several linked steps. The appearance of the front teeth alone cannot establish the required scope. Ask which movements are driving the recommendation and whether existing restorations, root positions or gum support limit the options.

02

Discuss supporting measures openly

Attachments, elastics, additional appliances or other space-management steps may be proposed depending on the assessment. The trays are only one part of the treatment. Ask what will be visible, what you must do each day and whether fixed braces would offer a more manageable alternative for the same objective.

03

Plan for review rather than a fixed tray forecast

Movement can differ from the planned sequence. Poor fit or unexpected bite changes need advice, and additional records or aligners may be considered. Clarify the included refinement scope and the point at which comprehensive treatment would be discussed. Retention remains necessary once the active movement is completed.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Coordinated planning can clarify how changes in different teeth affect the overall bite.

A defined goal allows progress and remaining limitations to be discussed before the final result is accepted.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

The middle category is not automatically suitable for every medium-looking problem.

Decay, gum problems, root changes and relapse remain possible; the package name does not remove orthodontic risks.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Follow the prescribed wearing and tray-change instructions, and ask about poor seating rather than advancing on your own.

Keep dental checks and cleaning appointments; use the agreed retainers after active treatment.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Will the treatment take an exact number of months?

No. Wearing consistency, biological response and changes in the plan affect timing.

Are refinements the same as failure?

Not necessarily. Additional aligners may respond to movement differing from the plan, but their reason and scope should be explained.

Can I move to the next tray despite a gap around a tooth?

Ask the team for advice before advancing; fit needs to be assessed.

What costs should I clarify?

Check additional scans, aligners, attachments, any other appliances and retention rather than comparing only the initial tray package.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Which movements determine the recommended scope?
  • What additional measures might be needed?
  • How are refinements and changes in cost handled?
