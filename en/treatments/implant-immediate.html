Consultation

Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan immediate implant placementCan the gap be treated when the tooth is removed?

Having a tooth extracted can bring worry about appearance, eating and an extra operation later. The possibility of placing an implant at the same visit may sound like a way to reduce that uncertainty. It is useful to separate three decisions: whether the tooth needs removal, when an implant can be placed and when a replacement tooth may take chewing force.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Extraction and implantation are separate decisions

The remaining tooth must first be assessed for preservation. If extraction is appropriate, the socket, surrounding bone and gums, infection and the planned implant position affect timing. A consultation can outline possibilities, but final conditions after removal may change whether immediate placement is appropriate.

02

Immediate placement is not immediate chewing

An implant placed on the extraction day may still need protected healing before loading. A visible temporary tooth can also be kept out of heavy contact. Ask exactly what the temporary restoration will do, what you may eat and when the final restoration will be assessed. The word “immediate” does not describe every stage.

03

Prepare a delayed-placement alternative

If suitable stability or tissue conditions cannot be achieved, waiting may be the appropriate decision. Discuss temporary options and whether tissue management or grafting is needed. Knowing this backup before extraction reduces the chance of feeling that a necessary change is a broken promise.

Potential benefits

Combining appropriate stages may reduce separate surgical appointments.

Temporary appearance and eating needs can be planned before extraction.

Limitations and risks

Immediate placement does not guarantee freedom from grafting or additional treatment.

Integration failure, infection and changes in gum contour remain possible.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Follow the loading restrictions for the actual temporary tooth provided.

Attend healing reviews even if the gap looks filled and pain has settled.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Can an infected tooth always receive an immediate implant?

No. Infection, remaining tissues and achievable implant stability require individual assessment.

Will the gum shape stay unchanged?

That cannot be guaranteed. Tissue changes after extraction and healing must be considered in the restorative plan.

Is delayed placement a failure?

No. Delaying may be a planned response to unsuitable conditions or a need for tissue healing.

What should the estimate show?

Separate extraction, implant placement, any grafting, temporary restoration and final tooth, including possible changes.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Why is immediate placement suitable for this socket?
  • What exactly will the temporary tooth allow me to do?
  • What is the alternative if conditions change?
