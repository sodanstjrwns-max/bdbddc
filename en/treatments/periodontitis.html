Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan periodontitis treatmentAre you afraid that one loose tooth means you will lose them all?

Being told that the bone around teeth has reduced can feel frightening, especially if pain gave you little warning. You may wonder why careful brushing was not enough or whether extraction is inevitable. Periodontitis care considers the support of each tooth and the conditions that make inflammation difficult to control.

01 / The questions behind the treatment name

You deserve to understand what this means for your life.

01

Is periodontitis the same as gums bleeding?

Bleeding is a sign to assess; periodontitis involves damage to supporting tissues.

02

Can scaling alone keep the teeth safe?

The required depth of treatment and the response must be assessed individually.

03

Is recurrence proof I did not try hard enough?

No. Technique, anatomy and other risk factors need review together.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

A measured treatment plan can address inflammation and assess tooth preservation.

What to weigh up

Support loss may remain and maintenance is an ongoing responsibility.

The question that helps you choose

Base choices on tooth-level findings, response and the ability to control recurring risks.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Distinguish bleeding from loss of support

Inflammation confined to the gums and disease affecting supporting tissues are not identical. Pocket measurements, imaging where appropriate and mobility help assess the extent. Ask which teeth have a reasonable preservation outlook and which are uncertain. A single loose tooth does not establish the same prognosis for the entire mouth.

02

Review the response to nonsurgical care

Plaque control and treatment of the root surfaces are commonly part of the initial approach. Reassessment identifies persistent deep or inflamed areas. Depending on the findings, additional treatment or surgery may be discussed. Repeating only a surface cleaning without understanding the remaining disease can leave uncertainty unresolved.

03

Manage recurrence without reducing it to blame

Smoking, medical conditions, difficult anatomy and restoration contours may affect control alongside home care. Already lost support cannot always be restored. The purpose of maintenance is to monitor the condition and respond to change, using a routine you can sustain rather than promising a one-time cure.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Early understanding of support loss helps prioritise treatment and follow-up.

Reassessment can identify where further intervention is justified.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Not all lost bone or gum support can be recovered.

Some sites may remain at risk despite treatment and require closer monitoring.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Use the recommended cleaning method and tell the team when a tool cannot reach comfortably.

Attend maintenance and report new looseness, swelling or pus rather than waiting for pain.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Do all mobile teeth need extraction?

No. The degree and cause of mobility, support and response to care must be assessed for each tooth.

Will regenerative material rebuild everything?

No. Suitability and expected benefit depend on the defect and clinical conditions.

Can implants solve the gum disease problem?

Replacing teeth does not remove the need to control inflammation and maintain implants and remaining teeth.

How often will I need reviews?

The interval should reflect your disease findings, response and risk factors rather than a single rule for everyone.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • What is the preservation outlook for each affected tooth?
  • Which sites remain after initial treatment?
  • What risks can we change and what limits remain?
