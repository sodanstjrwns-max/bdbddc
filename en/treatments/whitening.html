Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan teeth whiteningDo you want a brighter smile without making sensitive teeth worse?

You may notice yellowing in photographs or feel that whitening products have made little difference. If your teeth already react to cold, stronger products can feel like a risk rather than an answer. Start by understanding the cause of the colour and whether your visible teeth are natural teeth, fillings or crowns.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

Whitening can improve some natural-tooth discolouration without covering the tooth with a veneer.

What to weigh up

Sensitivity, gum irritation and uneven responses can limit the result.

The question that helps you choose

Identify the cause of the colour and coordinate natural teeth with existing restorations.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Check the source of the colour

Surface staining and changes within a tooth are not the same problem. A single dark tooth, a previous injury or a root-treated tooth may require a different assessment from general yellowing. Gum disease, decay and existing sensitivity should be considered before deciding whether whitening is appropriate.

02

Set a realistic shade goal

Natural teeth and restorations do not respond in the same way. A crown or filling may remain a different shade after surrounding teeth become lighter. Discuss the whole visible smile and whether any later restoration changes would be needed. A photograph or shade chart cannot guarantee a particular individual result.

03

Balance the method with comfort and maintenance

The method and schedule should fit your dental condition and tolerance. Sensitivity or gum irritation can occur, and using more product does not necessarily improve the result safely. Ask what symptoms should prompt a pause or review. Colour can change again over time, so discuss maintenance without assuming an unlimited permanent effect.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Some colour concerns may be addressed without preparing the tooth for a restoration.

An assessed plan can distinguish removable stain from deeper colour changes.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Not every type of discolouration responds equally.

The result is not permanent, and existing crowns or fillings may remain a different shade.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Follow the supplied method and stop to seek advice if symptoms exceed the agreed guidance.

Avoid adding multiple whitening products or extending treatment without advice.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is a stronger product always better?

No. The appropriate product and schedule depend on the condition of the teeth and risk of irritation.

Can whitening replace treating decay?

No. Disease and damaged restorations need their own assessment and treatment.

How white can my teeth become?

An exact shade cannot be guaranteed. Discuss realistic change from your starting colour.

Should I whiten before a new front crown?

Colour planning should be coordinated so the final restoration matches the intended natural-tooth shade.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • What is causing this particular discolouration?
  • How will existing crowns or fillings affect the result?
  • What should I do if sensitivity develops?
