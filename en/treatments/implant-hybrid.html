Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan hybrid full-arch implants (all-on-x)Before replacing a whole arch, can we talk about what frightens you?

A denture that lifts during conversation can make a fixed set of teeth sound like relief. But the thought of losing the teeth you still have may feel irreversible. You may also worry about being left without teeth, cleaning underneath a bridge, or paying again if one implant develops a problem. These are central planning questions.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

A connected fixed restoration can reduce movement associated with a loose removable denture.

What to weigh up

Extraction is irreversible, and underside cleaning and connected repairs can be demanding.

The question that helps you choose

Compare preservation, fixed designs and overdentures using your actual anatomy and daily care ability.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Preservation comes before the implant count

All-on-4 and All-on-6 describe approaches to supporting an arch, not a universal prescription. Assess each remaining tooth before deciding on extraction. Bone distribution, the opposite teeth, prosthetic space and the shape of the planned restoration influence support. More implants are not automatically better, and a named technique does not guarantee that grafting can be avoided.

02

Fixed teeth and removable options solve different problems

A hybrid restoration may replace teeth and some lost gum contour. It remains in place during daily use, but its underside needs accessible cleaning. Compare it with segmented fixed restorations and an implant overdenture. A removable option may suit cleaning or lip-support needs better. Ask to understand the maintenance routine before choosing the appearance.

03

Temporary teeth, speech and future repairs

Immediate fixed temporary teeth depend on surgical conditions and initial stability. A backup plan is needed when immediate loading is unsuitable. Temporary teeth also provide an opportunity to discuss speech, appearance and bite before the final restoration. A loose screw, chipped tooth and loss of implant support require different responses; ask which parts can be repaired independently.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Several missing teeth can be addressed within one coordinated restoration plan.

Tooth and gum contours can be considered together where tissue has been lost.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Implant integration problems and peri-implant disease remain possible.

Fractures, screw loosening and changes in speech or cleaning access may require adjustment or repair.

Treatment and everyday life

Four things to clarify together

  1. 01

    Explain the difficult moments

    Describe when you notice the problem, what you hope will change, and any previous experience you do not want to repeat.

  2. 02

    Compare realistic choices

    Ask which choices are suitable after examination, what each can and cannot change, and whether another problem needs attention first.

  3. 03

    Understand the stages

    Separate assessment, active treatment, healing or adaptation, and follow-up. Ask what would make the plan change.

  4. 04

    Plan ongoing care

    Check home care, review appointments and a route to advice. Long-term care can remain important after active treatment ends.

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Practise the proposed cleaning method and discuss whether help from a caregiver is needed.

Follow the prescribed diet during healing; a temporary bridge is not permission for unrestricted chewing.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Are four implants always enough?

No fixed number suits every jaw. Their distribution, bone conditions, opposing teeth and restoration design must be assessed together.

Will fixed teeth feel exactly like natural teeth?

That cannot be promised. Sensation, shape, speech and cleaning can differ, and adaptation or adjustments may be needed.

Is the final bridge fitted on the surgical day?

A temporary restoration and the final restoration are different stages. Their timing depends on healing and the agreed plan.

What should a full-arch estimate include?

Clarify extractions, grafting, temporary and final restorations, follow-up and the conditions and costs of repairs.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • Why is each remaining tooth being kept or removed?
  • How will I clean beneath this exact design?
  • What is the backup plan if immediate teeth are unsuitable?
