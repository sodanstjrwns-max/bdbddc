Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan hybrid full-arch implants (all-on-x)When teeth are missing, can eating feel more secure than it does with a loose denture?

Choosing food can become an exercise in avoiding what you cannot chew. If a denture lifts when you speak or eat, a shared meal or conversation may feel uncomfortable too. You may want teeth that stay in place, while worrying about surgery, eating during healing, and caring for a fixed bridge afterwards. Full-arch planning begins with daily life when an upper or lower jaw has no teeth.

01 / The questions behind the treatment name

You deserve to understand what this means for your life.

01

With no teeth left in this jaw, can I have teeth that stay in place?

A full-arch restoration uses several implants to support a connected fixed bridge. It may reduce movement associated with a loose denture, but bone, bite and cleaning conditions must be assessed. Natural-tooth sensation and unrestricted chewing cannot be promised.

02

What will I wear and eat while everything heals?

Immediate fixed temporary teeth depend on implant stability and surgical conditions. Discuss a backup temporary option, whether your existing denture can be used, and the prescribed diet before surgery.

03

How do I clean underneath, and what happens if something breaks?

The bridge needs accessible cleaning spaces and a daily routine. A loose screw, chipped restoration and inflamed implant tissues are different problems. Ask how this design can be maintained and repaired; do not remove or tighten it yourself.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

A fixed full-arch bridge can help restore chewing in a toothless jaw and reduce movement that makes meals and conversation difficult with a loose denture.

What to weigh up

Surgery, healing and early diet restrictions are followed by daily underside cleaning and professional review. Inflammation, loose screws or damaged prosthetic teeth may need treatment or repair.

The question that helps you choose

Compare fixed full-arch teeth and an implant overdenture using bone support, bite, speech, lip support, cleaning ability and the long-term care plan.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Start with life without teeth in this jaw

You may want to share the same meal as your family or speak without worrying that a denture will lift. Explain which moments matter most, and bring your current denture if you use one. These details help define what the treatment should try to improve.

All-on-X restores a toothless upper or lower arch with a bridge supported by several implants. It does not mean placing one implant for each missing tooth. The two jaws are planned individually, including how the new teeth will meet the teeth or denture opposite them.

02

The supporting layout matters more than a number

All-on-4 and All-on-6 describe approaches, not a universal prescription. Bone distribution, the opposing teeth, prosthetic space and the planned bridge influence implant placement. More implants are not automatically better, and a named technique does not guarantee that grafting can be avoided.

Ask why the proposed positions and number suit your jaw and how loss of one support could affect the bridge. The final restoration and access for cleaning need to be planned with the surgery.

03

Fixed teeth and removable options solve different problems

A hybrid restoration can include prosthetic teeth and replacement gum contour. It stays in place during daily use, but its underside still needs cleaning. An implant overdenture can be removed by the patient and may suit cleaning or lip-support needs better. Compare maintenance as well as appearance and stability.

04

Temporary teeth, speech and future repairs

Immediate fixed temporary teeth depend on surgical conditions and initial stability. Discuss the backup if immediate loading is unsuitable, including what you can wear and eat while healing. A temporary bridge is not permission to chew without restrictions.

The temporary stage can help assess speech, appearance and bite before the final restoration. A loose screw, chipped tooth and loss of implant support require different responses; ask which parts can be repaired and what temporary option is available.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Several implants can support a fixed restoration for a toothless upper or lower arch, helping restore chewing and reduce problems from a moving denture.

Tooth and gum contours, lip support, speech and cleaning access can be considered together in the restoration plan.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Implant integration problems and peri-implant disease remain possible.

Fractures, screw loosening and changes in speech or cleaning access may require adjustment or repair.

Fixed full-arch treatment for an edentulous jaw

This follows an edentulous arch where several implants support a full-arch restoration. Implant number, positions and provisional timing depend on bone and bite conditions.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Can a full-arch restoration be considered when a jaw has no teeth?

Yes. A fixed full-arch bridge is a way of restoring a toothless upper or lower jaw using several implants. Suitability depends on bone, soft tissues, overall health, prosthetic space and the ability to clean and maintain it.

Are four implants always enough?

No fixed number suits every jaw. Their distribution, bone conditions, opposing teeth and restoration design must be assessed together.

Will fixed teeth feel exactly like natural teeth?

That cannot be promised. Sensation, shape, speech and cleaning can differ, and adaptation or adjustments may be needed.

Is the final bridge fitted on the surgical day?

A temporary restoration and the final restoration are different stages. Their timing depends on healing and the agreed plan.

What should a full-arch estimate include?

Clarify examination, surgery, grafting, sedation, temporary and final restorations, follow-up and repair conditions. Check whether the estimate covers one jaw or both and how a change of plan would affect the cost.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • How do fixed full-arch teeth and an implant overdenture compare for my toothless jaw?
  • Why do these implant positions and this number suit my bone and planned bridge?
  • What is the backup plan for eating and temporary teeth if immediate loading is unsuitable?
  • Can you show me how I would clean beneath this exact design?
  • What are the repair options, temporary arrangements and costs if a screw or part of the bridge develops a problem?
