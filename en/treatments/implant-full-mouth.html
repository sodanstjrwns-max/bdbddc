Before choosing treatment, start with what worries you

Cheonan full-mouth implant rehabilitationWhen a jaw has no teeth, how can you eat with a stable set of teeth again?

You may avoid foods you enjoy or worry that a denture will move when eating with other people. Full-mouth implant rehabilitation plans support for replacement teeth in an edentulous upper or lower jaw. Understanding temporary teeth, healing and daily cleaning matters as much as the operation itself.

01 / The questions behind the treatment name

You deserve to understand what this means for your life.

01

Can implants support teeth in an edentulous jaw?

Suitability depends on bone, gum tissue, general health and the ability to maintain the restoration. Grafting and fixed or removable alternatives are considered individually.

02

Is this the same as All-on-X?

Full-mouth rehabilitation can use different implant numbers and prosthetic designs. All-on-X connects a complete arch to several implants; the appropriate design depends on individual anatomy and function.

03

How will I eat before the final teeth are fitted?

The type and timing of temporary teeth and diet restrictions are planned for each stage. Receiving temporary teeth does not automatically mean they can withstand normal biting forces.

02 / Benefits and burdens

Benefits and burdens

What may improve

Implant-supported teeth can restore support for chewing, speech and appearance in a jaw without teeth.

What to weigh up

Surgery, healing, temporary arrangements, adjustment and continuing cleaning and review are required.

The question that helps you choose

Compare fixed and removable designs against jaw anatomy, cleaning ability and the plan for eating during treatment.

03 / Make sense of your own situation

What changes the choice of treatment?

01

Plan support in the jaw without teeth

Assessment considers bone volume, gum tissue, nearby nerves and the sinus, and the relationship between the jaws. Implant positions and the number and design of restorations are planned together. Upper and lower jaws may require different approaches. Lip support, speech and space for cleaning also influence the design.

02

Separate placement, healing and temporary teeth

Implants are placed according to the plan and their stability and healing are followed. Bone grafting, when needed, may change the sequence. Immediate fixed temporary teeth are not suitable for everyone. The temporary restoration and permitted chewing forces depend on surgical conditions and initial stability.

03

Fit the final teeth and learn how to care for them

After checking healing and support, the bite and tooth positions are recorded for the final prosthesis. Trial fitting helps assess speech, appearance and lip support; the fit, connections and bite are checked at delivery. Daily cleaning beneath the restoration and regular checks remain necessary. New movement, pain or damage needs professional review.

Benefits and burdens

Potential benefits

Implant-supported restorations provide a planned way to replace an edentulous arch.

The tooth arrangement, speech, bite and cleaning space can be considered together.

Benefits and burdens

Limitations and risks

Extensive treatment does not eliminate future gum or mechanical problems.

Connected restorations may need significant repair if part of the support changes.

Implant restoration of an edentulous arch

This follows an edentulous arch where several implants support a full-arch restoration. Implant number, positions and provisional timing depend on bone and bite conditions.

  1. 01

    Place supports in the edentulous arch

    Implants are placed in the toothless arch according to bone and prosthetic-space planning.

  2. 02

    Assess healing and connections

    Stability and healing guide connection preparation. Immediate provisional use requires a separate decision.

  3. 03

    Try in and fix the full arch

    Jaw relations, tooth arrangement, fit and bite are checked before full-arch fixation.

  4. 04

04 / Treatment and everyday life

Plan for the time between appointments, too.

Discuss cleaning ability, eyesight, hand movement and possible caregiver help.

Keep written treatment stages, product records and repair arrangements.

Costs, time and follow-up

Ask for a plan you can understand and manage.

What does the estimate include?

Ask which examinations, temporary stages, final treatment, retainers or maintenance, and repairs are included. The scope depends on your findings and chosen plan. A headline price cannot describe every person’s total cost. Ask how a change in the plan would be explained and agreed before it goes ahead.

How will visits fit into my life?

Bring previous records if you have them, your medication list and the practical limits on travel or time off work. Ask what each visit is for, what may change the timetable, and who to contact if something feels wrong. You do not need to promise to start treatment at the first conversation.

05 / Questions worth asking again

Uncertainty is a good reason to ask another question.

Is full-mouth the same as All-on-X?

No. All-on-X is one approach to a complete arch; a full-mouth plan may use different designs in each jaw.

Can all surgery happen on one day?

Suitability depends on the extent, health and local conditions. Convenience alone should not determine staging.

Will the temporary and final teeth look identical?

Not necessarily. Healing and feedback about function or appearance may lead to adjustments before the final design.

How do I compare total costs?

Compare the same teeth, procedures, temporary stages, final materials and ongoing care provisions.

Take these questions to your consultation
  • What support does my jawbone allow?
  • Why is this prosthetic design proposed for each jaw?
  • What temporary teeth and diet are planned during healing?
