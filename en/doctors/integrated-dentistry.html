Comprehensive Dentistry

Comprehensive DentistryWhat is it?
Comprehensive, whole-patient dental care

As a formal specialty within dental schools, it plans and coordinates all the dental treatments a patient needs.
Especially for patients with systemic conditions, those needing complex multidisciplinary care, and older adults we coordinate comprehensive care.

In Comprehensive Dentistry, the care we provide

Key roles of a Comprehensive Dentistry specialist

Comprehensive treatment planning

When cavities, gum treatment, prosthetic restorations, and Orthodontics are needed at the same time, we set the overall priorities and treatment sequence.

Dental care for patients with medical conditions

We manage safe dental treatment for patients with hypertension, diabetes, heart disease, osteoporosis, and other systemic conditions.

Coordinating multidisciplinary care

When multiple specialties—Orthodontics, Restorative Dentistry, Prosthodontics, and Oral Surgery—are involved, we coordinate and manage the entire process.

Older adults (Geriatric Dentistry)

We provide personalized dental care that accounts for age-related oral changes, multiple medications, and systemic conditions.

Integrated Dental Implant and prosthetic care

For full-mouth rehabilitation cases requiring multiple Dental Implants and prosthetic restorations, we develop a comprehensive treatment plan.

Sedation Dentistry

If you have severe dental anxiety or need a long procedure, you can be treated comfortably under sedation.

Who is a Comprehensive Dentistry specialist?

A specialist who designs a patient's overall oral health plan

Comprehensive Dentistry specialistAfter graduating from a 6-year dental program, at a university hospital's Comprehensive Dentistry department, completes 3 years of residency trainingand must pass a specialist examination to earn the credential.

During training, restorative dentistry, Prosthodontics, Periodontics, Orthodontics, Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery, and Oral Medicine are covered broadly, and in particular, the interplay between systemic disease and dental care and comprehensive treatment planningare emphasized.

Unlike specialties that focus on a single discipline, we consider the patient's overall oral condition and general health togetherto determine the optimal order and methods of treatment.
