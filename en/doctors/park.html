Dr. Sanghyun Park’s Story

A meticulous, attentive pediatric dentist

Why I became a dentist

Ever since I was young I was quick to learn anything I could do with my hands—and at the same time, when I saw someone in pain, my heart went out to them.

As I thought about a career that uses both dexterity and empathy, the answer naturally was dentistry.

Reducing fear, easing discomfort, and helping patients enjoy a more comfortable daily life after treatment suited me well. So I "Putting people at ease by doing what I do best"I'm always grateful that this is the life I get to live.

✦

Clinical Expertise

Currently, I provide comprehensive pediatric and adolescent dental care, with strong expertise in the surgical procedures and treatments required at this stage.

For growing patients, we don't just look at the teeth; we also consider developmental and psychological factors.

That's why in pediatric dentistry, beyond core techniques, the ability to read a child's feelings and a demeanor that turns tension into comfort are paramount. I feel my natural disposition helps a great deal here.

✦

Treatment Style

If I had to sum up my approach in one sentence,

"Explain kindly and thoroughly; treat quickly and comfortably."

For a child, unfamiliar treatment can be frightening, and for parents, worrisome. So I first explain slowly at the child's level and clearly walk parents through the flow of care.

When explanations are sufficient, anxiety decreases for both child and parent, leading to more stable care. That's why I carry out treatment as quickly and efficiently as possible to minimize the burden the child feels.

✦

Treatment Philosophy

The principle I value most in care is

that patients feel comfort and trust in the treatment room.

In pediatric dentistry, rather than simply doing the procedure well, instilling the feeling that 'This dental office is okay' is far more important.

A child's fear has to fade so not only the outcome but the entire dental experience remains positive. That's why I always observe a child's reactions closely and approach care carefully so it becomes a positive experience, not a hurtful memory.

✦

A Memorable Patient

The patient I remember most was a child I met through that very process. Their fear of the dentist was so great that they couldn't even open their mouth properly so it took a lot of time to calmly build trust.

By speaking gently, praising even small efforts, and creating an atmosphere where the child could find their own courage, at some point they began to accept treatment on their own.

On the day treatment finished, the child "I'll only come to this dental office"—the look on their face when they said that is still vivid. That one sentence continues to sustain me as a pediatric dentist.
✦

Advice for Patients

The advice I give most often is very basic yet the most important—

"Please make sure to floss before bed."

Whether child or adult, baby teeth or permanent teeth, that one bit of care right before sleep makes a big difference in prevention. The stronger your basics, the more stable the results after treatment.

✦

Current Interests

Lately, rather than 'treating after problems arise,' customized preventive care based on each patient's characteristicsis where I've been focusing my studies.

Cavities and gum disease vary depending on each person’s lifestyle, environment, and risk level, so every child needs individualized care. I am continually researching how to reflect this more precisely in treatment.

✦

Personal side

Colleagues describe me as "meticulous and detail-oriented—almost to a fault"they say. In pediatric dentistry, even the tiniest mistake can stay in a child’s memory for a long time, so attentiveness is one of my greatest strengths. I always do my best not to miss even the smallest changes.

Outside of work, time with my daughteris the center of my day. Watching her grow deeply shapes my approach as a pediatric dentist. The small changes she shows each day are my greatest joy and naturally add warmth to how I look after the children I meet in the clinic.

At Seoul BD Dental, there’s just one feeling we hope patients and children experience.

"The dentist is more comfortable than I expected—and this is a place I can come back to."

Reducing fear and providing reassurance,
that is the most important goal I want to achieve here.

Doctor Interview

A message from Dr. Sanghyun Park

As a pediatric dental specialist, when do you feel most rewarded?

When a child who was initially afraid of the dentist, after a few visits, says, "Hello, doctor!" and greets me first, I feel most rewarded. I believe my role is to instill positive dental experiences.

What do you consider most important in treating children?

Communicating at the child’s eye level. When we explain why treatment is needed and what tools we will use in ways a child can understand, cooperation improves greatly.

Any message for parents?

Most dental phobias form in childhood. If a child’s first experience at the dentist is good, lifelong oral care becomes easier. Start your child’s first dental visit at Seoul BD Dental.
