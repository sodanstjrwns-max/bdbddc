Orthodontics

OrthodonticsWhat is it?
A dental specialty that treats malocclusion of the teeth and jaws

Orthodontics is a recognized dental specialty, addressing irregular tooth alignment, malocclusion, and jawbone discrepanciesthrough diagnosis and treatment.
Beyond simply straightening teeth we also aim to improve functional occlusion and facial harmony.

Orthodontic Treatments

Key treatment areas overseen by an orthodontic specialist

Clear Aligners (Invisalign)

An orthodontic treatment that moves teeth discreetly using clear aligners. They are removable, making daily life more convenient.

Braces (Brackets)

Metal, ceramic, or self-ligating brackets are bonded to the teeth for precise correction. Effective even for complex malocclusions.

Non-surgical Orthodontics

Improves Class 3 malocclusion (prognathism), open bite, and more without surgery. Jaw position is corrected using orthodontic appliances alone.

Orthodontics for Growing Patients (Children & Teens)

Guides jaw growth and straightens the teeth for children and adolescents who are still growing. Starting at the right time is highly effective.

Surgical Orthodontics (Orthognathic)

When there is a severe jaw discrepancy, we collaborate with Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery to provide orthodontic care before and after surgery.

Retreatment and Retention Care

If relapse occurs after previous orthodontic treatment, we provide retreatment; managing retainers after finishing treatment is also part of orthodontic care.

What is an orthodontic specialist?

A professional qualification earned after graduating from dental school and completing additional training

An orthodontic specialistcompletes a 6-year dental program and then trains in a university hospital Orthodontics department for 3 years as a residentand must pass the specialist board exam to be certified.

During training craniofacial growth and development, biomechanics, occlusion, and orthognathic surgery are studied in depth, enabling treatment plans that consider overall facial skeletal harmony, not just simple tooth movement.

Orthodontic treatment typically takes 1~2 yearsand is a long-term process, so precise diagnosis and a structured plan before starting are essential.
